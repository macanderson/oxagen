## 1. What you are actually building (your intentions, stated back to you)

Stripped to one sentence:

> **Oxagen is the context layer for enterprise AI agents: a typed, auditable memory knowledge graph built from a company's connected systems, that emits actionable events and exposes every capability identically over MCP, API, and an in-app agent.**

The intentions I read in the corpus, in priority order:

- **Context as the product.** Not a chatbot, not a RAG library — the *substrate* that makes any agent (yours or the customer's) smarter because it has a typed, deduplicated, relationship-aware, provenance-tracked model of the customer's world.
- **MCP-first, no drift.** Every capability reachable the same way from MCP, the HTTP API, and the in-app agent. This is an architectural commitment in CLAUDE.md and your memory, and it is correct — it is what lets you be *infrastructure* rather than *an app*.
- **Self-improvement as a moat.** Evals + memory weighting + the graph getting better as it is used. The compounding asset.
- **Enterprise-grade from the substrate.** IAM, RBAC, audit, SOC2, data residency, BYO-knowledge-graph, BYO-keys. You are selling to companies that will not move without these.
- **Hybrid economics.** Credits as a currency (1 credit = 1¢), metered usage, BYOK, a 90% margin floor — so that cheaper models + great context is also a *better business*, not just a better product.

That is a genuinely differentiated, defensible thesis. The problem is not the thesis. The problem is focus.

## 3. Unbiased verdict

**What v1 got right:**
- The thesis. Context-as-moat is real and increasingly validated by the market.
- The three-surface / MCP-first architecture.
- Building the knowledge graph and ingestion engine at all — that is hard, differentiated work and you did a lot of it (241 core tickets).
- Treating security and SOC2 as first-class (even if reactively).

**What v1 got wrong:**
- **Breadth before depth.** It chased three products. Media generation and personal-finance tooling are not adjacent to "context layer for enterprise agents" — they are different businesses with different buyers, different margins, and (for media) brutal vendor churn and IP risk.
- **Owning what it should have rented.** 63 tickets maintaining a zoo of media providers, each of which broke independently (Imagen dunning, Runway 400s, ElevenLabs swaps). That is a maintenance liability disguised as a feature.
- **Premature go-to-market.** 18 partner-outreach tickets and a "Customer Success Agent" before the core was hardened.
- **Letting integrity bugs into the money path.** Credit TOCTOU and double-credit bugs are not ordinary bugs — they are margin leaks and trust failures.
- **Leaking PII into analytics.** Raw email to ClickHouse/PostHog is a legal liability, not a telemetry detail.

**The one-line verdict:** *The wedge is right; the discipline was wrong. v2's job is the same product with one-third the surface area and ten times the rigor on the core.*

### Spend MORE here

- **Context quality.** Enrichment, dedup, inferred-entity *precision*, the quality gate that keeps noise out of the live graph. Your agents are only as good as the graph; this is the product, not plumbing.
- **Evals.** The harness that *proves* cheaper-model-plus-better-context wins. It is your moat and your sales demo in one.
- **Security / SOC2 / data residency.** Move these from reactive to proactive. They are deal gates.
- **Pricing, packaging, and metering accuracy.** Money correctness is trust.
- **MCP developer experience.** Your distribution channel.

### Spend LESS here

- Media generation, financial tooling, the connector long tail, speculative capabilities, and any UI polish that precedes product-market fit.

## 5. The hybrid usage billing model

Your memory already fixes the unit: **1 credit = 1¢, bought in USD, US-only, no FX, 90% margin floor, BYOK metered at a flat fee.** The recommendation is a **three-part hybrid tariff**, because pure subscription underprices heavy AI usage (margin death) and pure usage terrifies enterprise procurement (unpredictable). Hybrid gives a predictable floor, usage upside, and margin protection.

1. **Platform subscription (the floor).** Per-workspace tier (with seats) that buys *access and assurance*: SSO, RBAC, audit, SOC2 posture, support SLA, baseline graph hosting + storage, and an **included monthly credit allotment.** This is the predictable line procurement wants and it covers your fixed value.

2. **Metered usage in credits (the variable).** Everything that has a marginal cost is metered and drawn from credits, at **cost + margin (90% floor)**:
   - LLM tokens (in/out, by model).
   - Tool / capability invocations.
   - Graph operations that cost real compute — enrichment, inference, dedup, large traversals.
   - Storage and retention beyond the included tier.
   - (If media survives at all: per-generation, passed through at margin.)

3. **Committed-use / enterprise (the expansion).** Annual commit for a discount, plus the things only enterprises pay for: data residency, BYO-graph, BYO-keys, dedicated support, higher rate limits.

**Why credits as the unit is strategically right:** they **decouple your price from vendor cost volatility.** When Anthropic or your GPU costs move, you re-rate credits-per-operation without re-papering a single contract. BYOK then becomes a clean lever: the customer brings their own model spend, you charge a **flat metering fee per call** (the OXA-1167 "2 credits/call" model) for the orchestration, context, and audit you provide around it.

**Non-negotiable guardrails (learned from v1's billing bugs):**
- **Append-only credit ledger, instrumented on every call**, with a hard floor at zero balance. v1 shipped credit TOCTOU and Stripe-retry double-credit bugs; in a usage model those are *direct* margin leaks and trust failures.
- **Per-workspace budgets, caps, and usage alerts** — surprise bills churn customers faster than outages.
- **Metering must be a first-class, tested, audited path**, not a side effect. "Instrument every surface" (OXA-1351) is a *billing-correctness* requirement, not just observability.

## 6. The business, from every angle

**Product.** Ruthless focus on the context layer. The `defineContract` capability model is the SSOT; three-surface parity is law; nothing ships that does not go through the contract + audit + meter path. Resist re-adding media and finance.

**Product marketing (positioning).** *"The memory and context layer for enterprise AI agents."* The narrative writes itself from your thesis: everyone has the same models → context is where you win or lose → Oxagen is the context engine (typed graph + decaying memory + evals), MCP-native. Differentiate explicitly:
- *vs. vector-DB "memory":* you offer **typed, relationship-aware, auditable context with provenance** — not an embedding blob.
- *vs. RAG frameworks/libraries:* you are the **substrate + runtime**, not a library the customer has to operate.
- *vs. point agents:* you make **their** agents better; you are infrastructure, not a competitor to their app.

**Marketing (demand).** Developer-led. Three engines: (1) the **MCP ecosystem** — be the best context server in every MCP client; (2) the **"ontology for AI agents" content cluster** that is already half-written in the v1 backlog; (3) **open-source plugins** (claude-code, cursor) as top-of-funnel. Land bottom-up with developers, expand top-down into the enterprise.

**Sales.** Two motions, one funnel:
- **PLG / self-serve** via MCP + a free credit tier (the 200→300 bump is already in the backlog) to get developers to *value* before a sales call.
- **Enterprise AE motion** gated on SOC2, SSO, RBAC, audit, BYO-graph, BYO-keys, data residency. Expansion = usage growth + seats + commit.

**Legal.** This is where v1's sprawl is most dangerous and where focus pays a dividend:
- **DPA + transparent sub-processor list** (your LLM vendors). Enterprises will ask on day one.
- **PII discipline at the substrate.** v1 leaked raw email/PII into ClickHouse and PostHog — a GDPR/CCPA *and* SOC2 liability. Fix it once, in the substrate, with redaction filters. (This is also why "instrument everything" must be PII-aware.)
- **Data residency + BYO-graph/keys** double as **liability reduction** and a sovereignty selling point — the customer's most sensitive data and model keys never leave their boundary.
- **Exiting media generation removes a whole class of copyright/IP/deepfake liability.** Another reason it is the right cut.
- **AI-specific terms:** no training on customer data; clear output ownership; OSS license hygiene for the plugins and connector manifest.

**Finance.** Margin discipline (90% floor), credit liability as deferred revenue, unit economics measured **per workspace**, COGS watched as `LLM + infra` — with BYOK explicitly shifting model COGS to the customer. The hybrid model exists to make "cheap models + great context" show up as **gross margin**, not just as a nice product property.

**Ops / Infra.** Cheap-first, AWS-leaning, us-central, per your standing infra memory. Honor the three-store boundary religiously (Neo4j = graph, Postgres = transactional, ClickHouse = append-only events) — v1's pain was partly stores doing each other's jobs. **Consolidate v1's GCP sprawl**, and reduce firefighting structurally by hardening the substrate *before* widening the surface.

## 7. The 0 → 1 roadmap (invariant rings, not a Gantt)

You asked for something that stays true for the lifetime of iterations from zero to one. A dated roadmap cannot do that — it is wrong by next quarter. An **invariant loop** can.

Everything Oxagen does, forever, is one cycle:

> **Connect → Contextualize → Reason → Act → Measure → Improve** — MCP-first, three-surface parity, security as a gate.

Build it as **concentric rings**, inside-out. You never skip a ring; you only deepen it.

- **Ring 0 — Substrate.** The graph + `defineContract` capability model + IAM/audit + billing meter + three-surface parity. *Nothing ships that does not pass through this ring.* This is "solve once, no drift."
- **Ring 1 — Context in.** Lighthouse connectors + the 5-stage ingestion pipeline + enrichment + the quality gate + memory. *Garbage in, garbage agent.*
- **Ring 2 — Reason & act.** Agent runtime: subagents, plans, skills, tools, code-exec. *Context that does nothing is a wiki.*
- **Ring 3 — Trust & improve.** Evals + self-improvement + observability + audit. *This is the ring that compounds — the moat.*
- **Ring 4 — Open & scale.** Partner-extensible connector manifest + BYO-graph/keys + enterprise hardening + ecosystem/marketplace. *You open the platform only after the inner rings are solid.*

**The invariants — true at 0.1 and at 1.0:**
1. Context is the moat; everything serves context quality.
2. Solve once; no drift across MCP / API / app.
3. Security and audit are gates, not bolt-ons.
4. Measure everything; the eval loop is the product getting better.
5. Cheap models + great context win — and that must show up in *margin*.
6. The customer owns their graph and their keys.

**Stage gates from 0 to 1** (the only "timeline" that survives contact with reality):

- **0 → 0.25** — Substrate + one lighthouse connector + an agent-runtime MVP usable end-to-end over MCP. **Dogfood it internally** (you already are, via this plugin).
- **0.25 → 0.5** — Memory + enrichment + the eval harness; 3–5 connectors; a handful of design partners using it for real.
- **0.5 → 0.75** — SOC2 + RBAC + BYO + data residency; first paying enterprise; hybrid billing live and *correct*.
- **0.75 → 1.0** — Partner-extensible connector manifest + marketplace; self-serve PLG funnel; a repeatable sales motion with **positive unit economics on the core and zero dependence on the cut surfaces.**

**"1" is not a feature count.** One is *a repeatable go-to-market with positive unit economics on the context layer alone.* If you get there still carrying media generation and financial tooling, you have not reached one — you have built three half-companies.

## 8. One page, if you read nothing else

- **Keep the thesis, cut the sprawl.** The context layer for enterprise AI agents is right. The media-gen product (63 tickets) and the finance product (41 tickets) are not — eliminate them.
- **The core is:** typed knowledge graph, decaying auditable memory, ingestion pipeline + connector *manifest*, agent runtime, evals, and an MCP-first contract surface with IAM/audit/SOC2.
- **Connectors are a manifest, not a treadmill.** 3–5 lighthouse integrations + partner-extensible registry.
- **Bill hybrid:** subscription floor + metered credits (90% margin floor) + enterprise commit, with BYOK at a flat per-call fee and a *correct, append-only, instrumented* ledger.
- **Security, PII discipline, and SOC2 are go-to-market, not hygiene.** v1 paid 97 firefighting tickets and a PII-leak liability for ignoring this. Do not repeat it.
- **Distribution is developer-led** through the MCP ecosystem, content, and OSS plugins; sales is PLG-to-enterprise.
- **The roadmap is a loop, not a list:** Connect → Contextualize → Reason → Act → Measure → Improve. Build it in rings, inside-out, and you stay true to it from 0.1 to 1.0.

*The hardest thing on this list is not building anything new. It is having the discipline to delete two products you already built. That deletion is the highest-leverage thing you can do before one.*
