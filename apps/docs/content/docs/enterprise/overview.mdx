## The organization and workspace model

Oxagen organizes every customer account as an **organization** with one or more **workspaces**.

```text title="Example structure"
Organization (Acme Corp)
├── Workspace: Marketing
├── Workspace: Engineering
├── Workspace: Finance
└── Workspace: Legal
```

This structure matches how most enterprises divide accountability for the agents they run:

- The **organization** is the accountable entity. It owns billing, sets the outer bounds of every rule, manages the member roster, and holds the audit log for the whole company.
- **Workspaces** are the operating units. Each team, department, or project has its own isolated workspace with its own agents, knowledge, and automation. Teams work on their own without seeing each other's data.

The database enforces workspace isolation (see [Tenant isolation and RLS](/docs/security/tenant-isolation-rls)), not application-layer conventions. A bug in an agent in the Marketing workspace cannot read Finance workspace data.

## Identity federation

SAML 2.0 and OIDC single sign-on and SCIM 2.0 directory sync are not available. The SSO and SCIM settings stay hidden under **Organization → Security** until their capability contracts ship, and the security page reports SSO enforcement as not configurable. Members sign in with email and password, Google, or GitHub, and you can require org-wide MFA for privileged roles on the MFA tab.

## Governance at scale

### Central IT sets the outer bounds

Every access decision is made by role: a principal's role grants first, then the system org Owner role, then the capability contract's default effect. The org decides who holds which role, so an org administrator controls access in every workspace, including workspaces created later.

  RG -->|deny · require approval · allow| DONE["Decision"]
  RG -->|none| OW{{"System org Owner?"}}
  OW -->|yes| ALLOW["Allow"]
  OW -->|no| DEF["Contract defaultEffect"]
`} />

The IAM resolver also defines two stronger tiers: org-enforced policies that workspaces cannot override, and workspace grants and denies that narrow access inside those bounds. Neither tier is active. Migration 0027 removed their tables, so no live request is decided by them.

### Agents as accountable principals

Every agent configuration is a principal with its own role grants. Each grant names a capability the agent may request and the answer: allow, deny, or require approval. A department grants what its task needs and nothing more. Triggered and scheduled agents run under their own grants. They never inherit the grants of the person who signed in.

### Approval-gated capabilities

When a role grant says a capability requires approval, the request is routed instead of run. The call returns `pending_approval`, and Oxagen records an access request (`arq_…`) that names the principal and the capability, beside the security event. The app has no screen for answering these requests, and there is no time-limited grant flow.
