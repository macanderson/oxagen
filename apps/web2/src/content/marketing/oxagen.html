The whole idea

Three moments, and Oxagen owns all three.

Governance for agents fails when it only shows up at one of them. A policy you can only check afterwards is a report. A log with no meter is a story about money you already spent.

Before

Approved before it runs

Decide who and what may take each action. Anything outside the rules simply does not run — so there is no exception to chase down later, and no apology to write.

During

Measured as it runs

Every model call and tool action is counted while it happens. You know what your agents cost by team, by task, and by the customer you bill — not at the end of the month.

After

On the record

A tamper-evident log of every action an agent took and every source it cited. Ready for your next security review instead of a scramble the week before it.

One shared map

The answer is a lookup, not a guess.

Connect your code and the systems around it once. Oxagen builds one connected map of how they relate, and every agent — in a terminal, in the app, over the API, in an editor — asks the same map and gets the same answer, with citations you can open.

  • —Scoped to the person asking. The map honours your existing permissions.
  • —Bounded results. An agent gets what it needs, not the whole company.
  • —Every answer cites its source, so a wrong one is traceable rather than mysterious.

Work, not prompts

Your tracker is the queue.

Agents should pick up the work you already wrote down. Oxagen connects to the tracker your team uses, so an agent's job starts as a real issue and finishes as a real pull request — with the plan, the evidence and the spend all attached to that issue.

A blocked issue says gate e2e red, not "the agent seems stuck".

The tools around it

Connect once. Every agent gets it.

Tool servers are configured in one place and inherited everywhere, with their transport, latency and auth state visible. A server that is slow shows up as a number — instead of an agent that feels sluggish for reasons nobody can name.

Institutional memory

What your team knows, kept where agents read it.

The conventions your reviewers keep repeating become small, versioned notes that travel with the repository. Every one is measured: how often it fired, and what it cost in tokens.

Which means the honest question — is this one earning its place? — has an answer instead of an opinion.

The short version

What the control plane holds

Oxagen sits beside your agents rather than inside them. It does not want your model keys and it never becomes the thing your work has to route through to function.
